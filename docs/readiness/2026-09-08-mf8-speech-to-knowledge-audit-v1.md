# MF-8 — the speech-to-knowledge audit: pricing the three-dimension direction (2026-09-08)

Row id `SAEA-SLURP-MF8-SPEECH-TO-KNOWLEDGE-AUDIT-V1`, profile `model-free-check`.
Receipts: `docs/checks/speech-aware-evidence-acquisition/2026-09-08-mf8-speech-to-knowledge-audit/`.
**Zero model contacts, zero GPU, zero tool, zero embedding, zero network, zero audio decode.**

## 0. What this audit is for

The owner's 2026-09-08 direction: *AI should understand audio intent directly; explore how
(multi-speaker) speech maps onto already-constructed knowledge in three dimensions —*

1. **text-domain knowledge** — the gold TRAIN text of an exemplar utterance;
2. **speech-domain knowledge** — *the same speaker's* semantically diverse expressions of one
   intent, as audio;
3. **state knowledge** — what a home assistant legitimately holds before the utterance, the part
   that raises business performance.

This note prices that direction before anything is preregistered. It answers five questions with
numbers and cites the source of every one. **It admits nothing, promotes nothing, and does not
preregister a cell.** Every cell sketched in section 5 is a *price*, not a registration.

## 0.1 Boundary, stated before the first read

* The held-out `test` split is never named, loaded, hashed or scored.
* **DEV sentence text is never read.** Only DEV *gold intent labels* enter, and only to COUNT
  exemplar availability and to attribute comparator errors. No runtime design in this note may
  read a DEV gold label; the availability counts are an audit statistic about the corpus, not a
  mechanism input, and section 5 states for each priced arm what it would be allowed to see.
* TRAIN gold text is read as **knowledge inventory** — it is construction data under the study's
  split-usage philosophy, and it is already the source of every knowledge receipt in this repo.
* No new speech identity opens. Every DEV-1000 row here has already been contacted; every TRAIN
  utterance read here is already inside the sealed knowledge-build closure.
* **The query stays speech-only.** Every arm priced here sends the row's own recording and nothing
  derived from it in text — no ASR, no transcript, no draft of the row's own words — under the
  owner's 2026-09-06 rule. What an exemplar contributes is TRAIN GOLD TEXT, which is *knowledge*
  about a different utterance the system already holds, never a transcript of the query. The two
  are separated by whose speech they describe, and any arm that blurs that line is out of scope.
* Audio *headers* (frame count, sample rate) may be read for duration arithmetic; **no audio
  sample is decoded** and no waveform is opened.

### 2.4 Candidate sets built WITHOUT the gold — the oracle-candidate price

The design being priced supplies a small candidate set and asks the core to MATCH speech against it,
so retrieval and matching are separated. A candidate set is only honest if it is built without the
gold label. Three sources were asked for; here is what each is worth.

**(a) The comparator's own top-k — DOES NOT EXIST.** The sealed outputs carry `text` and three
sha256 fields per row and nothing else; the string `logprob` occurs **zero** times in the 540 KB
outputs file and zero times in the 10 MB trace. The flight never set `decoding_params.logprobs`
(`core.model.request_types` gates the field on it), so no distribution was ever stored. Getting one
means re-flying 1,000 contacts — it is a *cost*, not an available asset, and this audit reports it
as absent rather than approximating it.

**(b) The PREDICTED scenario's legal intents**, ordered by TRAIN prior. Mean list size **3.47** on
the erroneous rows.

**(c) The pinned Nemotron speech-vector neighbours** (`q-speech-nemotron` and the input-fused

### 2.5 The deterministic legality judge — free, exact, and small

Source: `legality-judge.json`. The cheapest thing an agent system can put in front of a core
revise is a rule that reads the core's own output against the TRAIN-mined closed list and decides
whether to escalate. No gold, no model, no embedding.

| tier | rows flagged |
|---|---|
| predicted scenario is not a legal scenario name | **21** |
| action is illegal for the (legal) scenario named | **18** |
| response did not parse | **3** |
| **total flagged** | **42 of 1,000 (4.20 %)** |

* **Precision 100.00 %, false flags on correct rows: 0.** Not a measurement artefact — a structural
  fact this read verified rather than assumed: **zero** DEV-1000 gold intents lie outside the 60
  TRAIN pairs, so a row naming an illegal pair *cannot* be correct. The judge can never cost the
  system a right answer.
* **Recall over the raw errors: 14.69 %** (42 of the 286 errors the unprojected labels make).
* **17 of the 42 are already repaired for free** by `slurp-legal-label-projection-v2`; **25 remain
  wrong** and are the rows a core revise would actually be spent on.

**The honest reading is that the judge is exact and small.** It intercepts 4.2 % of rows at zero
risk and, after the projection that already ships, leaves 25 rows worth escalating — **2.5 % of the
roster and 9.3 % of the error mass**. A system built on legality alone tops out there. Its value is
not the 25 rows: it is that a 100 %-precision gate lets an expensive arm run on 4.2 % of rows
instead of 100 %, and the same escalate-only-when-flagged shape is what any *richer* judge
(candidate-set disagreement, neighbour-vote disagreement) would reuse. Those richer judges are not
measured here and are not proposed.

Note the inversion between the two populations: the **fused** key is the better candidate source on
all rows (93.40 at 8) and the **speech-only** key is better on the *error* rows (83.64 against
77.32). The fusion's state half is the arm's own draft, so on rows where the arm is wrong the state
is wrong too, and it pulls the neighbourhood toward the arm's mistake. **For a design whose whole
purpose is to repair the arm, the plain speech key is the right one** — which is also the order
KU-S3 registered, for a different reason.


## 3. Multi-audio prompt feasibility — VERDICT: both routes already carry it

Source reading against the PINNED build only: `/home/chao/llama.cpp-featcache` at
`04ccc72a2207347ca76161b93fd9d846dd0a8675`, the commit the runtime receipt names. **No server was
started, no model was loaded, no request was sent.** Cost arithmetic:
`multi-audio-feasibility.json`, from container headers alone.

### 3.1 The featcache PACKET route already binds N packets per request — and it was built to

This is the stronger finding, because it needs no new transport and no new patch.

**Study side.** `core/model/transport.py:712-771` reads `speech_feature_parts` as a **list**;
each entry must carry exactly `{packet_id, sha256, role}` with `role` in `{"query", "key"}`
(`:730-741`), every entry is validated by `load_speech_feature_packets` (`:749-761`), and the only
cardinality constraint is **exactly one `query`** (`:762-766`). `core/policies.py:192-221` enforces
the same shape at arm-construction time. So a request already means "one query clip plus any number
of KEY clips", and the word the code uses for a non-query audio packet is literally `key`.

**Server side, pinned build.** `tools/server/server-common.cpp`:
* `:1362-1387` — a `content[]` part of type `input_speech_features` is validated
  (exact `packet_id`/`sha256`/`role` keys, `role` in `{query,key}`), pushed onto
  `speech_feature_descriptors` (`:1384`) and **replaced by a media marker** (`:1385-1386`). One
  part, one marker; N parts, N markers.
* `:1408-1418` — after the content loop: exactly one `query` descriptor
  (`:1414`), and packets may not be mixed with raw media (`:1417`).
* `:1482-1483` — the descriptor array is forwarded to the slot as `speech_feature_descriptors`.
* `process_speech_feature_prompt` (`:996-1066`) loads **one packet per descriptor** (`:1021-1024`),
  then walks the prompt marker by marker and splices each packet's chunks in order
  (`:1039-1063`), raising if the prompt has more markers than descriptors (`:1052`) or fewer
  (`:1065`). `server-context.cpp:4269-4280` is the caller.

**Verdict: N audio clips per request on the packet route is a SUPPORTED, ALREADY-EXERCISED path**
— `rails/probes/pslu_flight.py:1982` builds `"role": "key"` descriptors, and
`tests/unit/test_llama_server_transport.py:470,543,791` covers multi-packet requests. What binds
today's SLURP cells to one packet is the CELL's own payload construction
(`core/policies.py:371` passes `[dict(query)]`), not the transport and not the server.

### 3.2 The raw-audio mmproj route also carries N clips

`tools/mtmd/mtmd.cpp:843-873` — the tokenizer splits the prompt on `ctx->media_marker`
(`:853-856`) and consumes **one bitmap per marker** (`:857-861`), then asserts marker count equals
bitmap count (`:866-873`). `tools/server/server-common.cpp:1347-1361` turns every OpenAI
`input_audio` content part into a marker and appends its bytes to `out_files`, so an N-clip chat
request is N markers and N bitmaps; `process_mtmd_prompt` (`:699-726`) is where they meet. The
interleaving is free: text may sit between clips, because the split is on marker positions.

**So the mechanism is not blocked at any layer.** What differs is COST: the raw route pays one
mmproj encode per clip per request, while the packet route reuses a pre-materialized packet, which
is exactly the featcache's reason to exist. For a design that shows the SAME exemplar clip to many
rows, the packet route is strictly better — each exemplar is encoded once, ever.

### 3.3 The encoder rate, from the source rather than from folklore

`tools/mtmd/clip.cpp:3398-3403`, `PROJECTOR_TYPE_QWEN3A`:
`chunk_size = 100` mel frames, `tokens_per_chunk = 13`,
`n_patches = (nx / chunk_size) * tokens_per_chunk`. Mel runs at 100 frames per second, so
**13 prompt tokens per second of audio, allocated in whole one-second blocks** — which is the same
constant the Earnings-21 tail probe measured on the wire (2.0 s of appended silence cost exactly
1,593 × 26 tokens, no residue).

### 3.4 The cost, measured

DEV-1000 query clips: 2,884.7937 s total, **44.16 audio tokens per row at the mean** (median 39,
p90 65, max 169). The sealed comparator's realized prompt is **2,190.16 tokens per row**, so the
fixed instruction is **2,146 tokens** and the query audio is 2 % of the prompt.

The same-speaker exemplar pool actually available to this design — every TRAIN recording whose
(speaker, intent) matches some DEV-1000 row's (speaker, gold intent) — is **15,556 recordings over
823 distinct (speaker, intent) pairs**, 42,771.36 s in total, **2.75 s / 42.1 tokens at the mean**
(median 39 tokens, p90 65, max 247).

| k exemplar clips | added audio tokens (mean) | prompt tokens (mean) | prompt tokens (worst case) | slot headroom at −c 65536 −np 8 | fits 8,192-token slot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | **126.4** | 2,316.5 | 3,056 | **3.54×** | yes |
| 5 | **210.6** | 2,400.8 | 3,550 | **3.41×** | yes |
| 8 | **337.0** | 2,527.1 | 4,291 | **3.24×** | yes |

**Every k this audit was asked about fits the existing SLURP geometry with more than 3× headroom,

**NOT token-matched by construction**. Under the owner's 2026-09-06 ruling the lock is a form twin
rather than a token count, so this does not sink the twin — but the modality contrast has a
confounded prompt volume, it must be reported as a covariate, and a token-matched variant would
have to pad the text arm rather than trim the audio one.
